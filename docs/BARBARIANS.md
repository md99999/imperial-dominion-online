# Barbarians

A rogue element: raiders who appear without warning during a turn, plunder a share of an empire's
gold and grain, and leave. Nobody sends them and nobody can fight them. They are weather.

## What they do

When a turn is spent, there is a chance the barbarians arrive. If they do, they take a share of
the gold and a share of the grain in the treasury at that moment, and the ruler is told plainly:

> Barbarians have raided your empire and carried off 12,480 gold and 3,100 grain.

The raid also reaches the gazette, because a leader being humbled is news and the rest of the
board enjoys it.

## Who they visit, and when

Two conditions, and both matter more than the percentages.

**Only the top three empires on the scoreboard.** Barbarians are a brake on a runaway leader, not
a tax on everybody. An empire that has pulled ahead pays a small, unpredictable price for being
ahead, and the empires chasing it do not. That makes the lead worth having and expensive to hold,
which is the point.

**Only when ten or more empires are playing.** On a small board the top three is most of the
board, and the mechanic stops being a brake and becomes a tax on the only people playing. Below
the threshold barbarians simply do not exist.

Both the threshold and the percentages are settings. The defaults are ten percent of gold, ten
percent of grain, on a one in twenty chance per turn spent.

## Why a share rather than a number

A flat raid punishes a small empire and tickles a large one. A share scales with what is there:
the leader loses more in absolute terms precisely because they have more, which is the behaviour
wanted from a catch-up mechanic. It also means the raid can never bankrupt anybody, since ninety
percent of something is still something.

## In league play

Barbarians stay active. A league does not make the countryside safe, and the same brake on a
runaway leader is worth having when the leader is helping a site win a season.

The scoreboard consulted is the **local** one, not the league table. The local standings are what a
site's own players see and compete on, and a raid explained by a table from another site would read
as arbitrary.

## What they are not

They do not take land, buildings, troops or siege weapons. They are not an attack and produce no
battle report, there is nothing to defend against, and no defence reduces them. Fortifications, an
army at home, a crown truce and a grace period make no difference: barbarians ignore all of it.

That is deliberate. The moment they can be defended against, they become another military
calculation, and the game already has one of those. They are a cost of being in front.
